01

Electrolytes First. Always.

Every RTD cocktail in India is sugar water with alcohol. We said no. We built our base around 220mg of electrolytes — sodium, potassium, magnesium — so the drink actually does something good for you while you party. Nobody else tried it. We did it first.

02

Desi Flavours Are Premium.

Kokum. Tamarind. Hibiscus. Cardamom. Kala namak. These are not "ethnic" — these are world-class ingredients that Indian craft bars charge ₹800 to use. We put them in a can and sold them on a cart. Nostalgia and nightlife, same sip.

03

The Cart Is the Brand.

We don't want a bar. We want a cart. No reservation, no dress code, no pretension. The cart shows up where the party already is — markets, gigs, rooftops, parking lots. We come to you. That's the whole point.

Chapter Two
The Brand
The Logo

The BUURP wordmark is the brand's primary identity asset. The double-U with the bubble rising through it is not a design flourish — it is the product in typographic form. Carbonation. Energy. Chaos. Rising. The pink offset shadow gives it weight and irreverence simultaneously.

Primary — Dark
On Brand Colour
✓ Do
  • Use on pure black or very dark backgrounds always
  • Give it breathing room — minimum clear space = height of the B
  • Use mix-blend-mode: lighten when placing on coloured backgrounds
  • Let it be big and proud — this mark earns its space
  • Use the full wordmark in all brand contexts
✗ Don't
  • Never place on white or light backgrounds without treatment
  • Never stretch, compress, or rotate the mark
  • Never use a drop shadow — the offset pink is already the shadow
  • Never recreate in a different font — Bebas Neue is not BUURP
  • Never crop the bubble detail — it is load-bearing
Colour System

BUURP's palette is built for night. Every colour reads on a dark background. Every colour has a cultural reference point. Lime is energy and irreverence. Pink is aggression and femininity. Orange is heat. Blue is liberation. Black is the stage everything performs on.

Chapter Four
Tone of Voice
How We Talk

BUURP doesn't market. It speaks. The brand voice is built from three qualities — not three rules. Break the rules as long as you keep the feeling.

01
Honest. Never Humble.
We say what we mean. We don't over-explain. We don't apologise. We don't say "we believe" — we say "we know." Confidence without arrogance. Swagger without cringe.
"We put electrolytes in. No one else did."
"Vibes in. Regrets optional."
02
Irreverent. Never Stupid.
The brand leans into the absurdity of its name without becoming a joke. The humour is dry and knowing — like the smartest person at the party who also happens to be the loudest.
"Nah I'm 12." (age gate decline button)
"Go drink a juice box, kid. 🧃"
03
Desi. Not Apologetically.
We use Hindi, Kannada, Tamil without translation. We reference kokum without explaining what it is. The brand assumes cultural fluency. If you know, you know. If you don't — welcome, catch up.
"AAO. PEEVO. GAAO."
"RAA. TAAGU. PAADU."
Writing It Right
✓ Write like this
  • Short sentences. Punchy. Then stop.
  • Use full stops aggressively. Even in the middle.
  • Mix Hindi/regional naturally — don't italicise it
  • Name the flavours like they're sacred: Moksha. Soma. Rasa. Tatva.
  • Talk about the cart like it's a character, not a product
  • "Find the cart." Not "purchase at our location."
✗ Not like this
  • "We are excited to announce our new product launch"
  • Excessive exclamation marks!!! It's desperate.
  • Translating desi references in parentheses (kokum, a sour fruit)
  • "Enjoy responsibly" as a standalone sentence — always pair it with irreverence
  • Wellness language: "nourishing", "wholesome", "clean"
  • Anything that sounds like a press release
